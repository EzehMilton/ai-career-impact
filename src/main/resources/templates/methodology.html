Your information

If you upload a CV

PDF or DOCX parsing first extracts text deterministically. A separate AI step turns that text into a small professional profile for you to review and correct. It extracts information only: it does not score the work, predict job loss or make labour-market claims. The confirmed profile then enters the same assessment steps as manual input.

Our process

The steps

Each assessment follows the same four steps.

  1. 01

    Structured AI judgments

    An AI model answers a fixed set of small, specific questions about what you described — for example how repeatable a task is, or how much quantitative reasoning a subject combination builds. Each question has a defined scale with every level written out in advance. The model never produces an overall verdict.

  2. 02

    Deterministic policy in code

    Ordinary Java then combines those judgments using fixed, published weights and thresholds. This is where your measures, bands and explanation come from. It is the same arithmetic every time, so the same judgments always give the same result.

  3. 03

    A written explanation

    A second AI step turns the already-decided result into readable prose. It is told that the saved numbers are final and may not be recalculated, contradicted or restated numerically. If it strays, the explanation is discarded — your assessment is unaffected.

  4. 04

    External context, kept separate

    Where we have a traceable external source we show it in its own section with its publisher and date. Evidence never changes a measure.

Different questions, different measures

Why the three assessments are not comparable

The professional, university and A-level assessments measure genuinely different things, so their numbers mean different things:

Professional

AI Exposure

describes how readily the work you already do might be performed, automated or changed by AI. It is not a probability of job loss.

University

Future Readiness

describes how well a course appears to develop capabilities that stay valuable as AI tools improve. It is not a guarantee of employability.

A-level

Future Optionality

describes how wide a range of plausible next steps a subject combination keeps open. It is not a guarantee of university admission or career success.

Each uses its own questions, its own weights and its own wording for bands. A 7.2 in one assessment has nothing to do with a 7.2 in another, and there is deliberately no single overall AI score anywhere in this application.

About the weights

The weights and thresholds are provisional product policy. They are a considered, transparent starting point — not validated educational or economic science, and not the view of any employer, university or admissions body. Each set carries a version, so a saved result always keeps the methodology that produced it. If we change the weights, we publish a new version rather than quietly altering what an old result meant.

Your result does not change later

An assessment is stored as a snapshot. Re-opening it shows exactly what was produced at the time, with the methodology version that produced it. Nothing is silently re-scored, and re-opening a page never re-runs the assessment.

Clear from the start

What the free assessment and the full report contain

Both use the same assessment. The free result gives you the measures, the main factors behind them and a short interpretation. The full report adds a deeper explanation of each measure, practical next steps, skill guidance, external context where we have it, and a PDF. Paying unlocks depth and explanation — it does not buy a more accurate or more favourable score.

Our boundaries

What we deliberately do not do

  • We do not predict whether you will lose a job, get an offer or succeed in a career.
  • We do not rank subjects, courses, universities or employers.
  • We do not invent statistics. Salary and labour-market figures only ever appear with a source.
  • We do not invent university entry requirements. Subject requirements are facts to check on the course's own page, and we say so rather than guessing.
  • We do not tell you to change your subjects, abandon a course or leave a career.
